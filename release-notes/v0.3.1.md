# StreamDek TV v0.3.1

## What's New

- **StreamDek TV speaks eight languages.** English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese,
  Dutch and Polish, with Hindi coming soon. Under **Settings → Appearance**, choose **App language**
  and the whole interface changes as you press OK — no restart, no signing out, nothing to back out
  of. It survives closing the app, restarting the television and updating it.

  **App language is not your audio or subtitle language.** They are three separate settings and
  they stay separate: a viewer can reasonably read a Polish interface while listening to English
  audio with French subtitles, and collapsing any two of them together would eventually produce
  playback nobody asked for. Preferred audio and preferred subtitle language are where they always
  were, under **Playback**, and neither is touched by this.

  The choice is device-local. It is deliberately the one preference that does not sync: a
  television in a shared room and a phone in one person's hand can reasonably want different
  answers, and syncing it would mean one member of a household silently changing the language for
  everyone else. Your phone keeps its own.

  Languages are listed under their own names — Français, Deutsch, Español, Polski — so a viewer who
  picks the wrong one by mistake can still find their way back. Nothing else in the list is
  translated for the same reason.

- **What is translated, and what deliberately is not.** Every word StreamDek itself writes is
  translated: menus, settings, buttons, empty states, error messages, the stream picker, the player
  panels, the sleep timer, the Home rows editor and every confirmation you can be shown.

  Film and series titles, cast and crew names, genres, synopses, channel and category names, add-on
  and provider names, release names and file names are **not**. Those belong to TMDB, to your
  add-ons and to your providers — translating them would be inventing something the source did not
  say, and would break the matching that finds a subtitle in the language you asked for. A German
  interface shows German menus around the titles the sources actually published.

## Improvements

- **Numbers, dates and durations are written the way your language writes them.** A rating shows as
  **7,8** in French and **7.8** in English. A channel count is grouped as **12 345** or **12,345**
  depending on where you are. A running time is **2 h 15 min** in French and **2 Std. 15 Min.** in
  German. A new episode that landed on the 31st of August says **31 août**, not **31 Aug**.

  These went through the interface language rather than the television's own locale, which is the
  distinction that matters: a French interface on an English-region set was previously writing
  English numbers and English month names inside otherwise French sentences.

- **Sentences are translated as sentences.** Nothing on screen is assembled from fragments joined
  together, because the order those fragments belong in is different in every language. Anything
  with a number in it — "3 of 17 active", "12 sources still loading", "cleared 2 hours ago" —
  carries a proper plural rule for each language, including the three-form rules Polish needs and
  the separate forms French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese use.

- **Layouts take the longer words.** German and Polish routinely run twenty to fifty per cent
  longer than English. Headings, chips, rows and buttons were sized for that rather than for the
  English they were written in, and long page titles scroll rather than pushing the controls beside
  them off the screen.

## Fixed

- **Home loading itself twice on a cold start.** Opening the app went to Home, filled it in, and
  then visibly reloaded the whole screen — twice — before settling. The screen decides when to
  reload by building a key out of which add-ons are enabled and in what order; that key was built
  from the add-on list in whatever order it happened to arrive in, and the same add-ons arriving in
  a different order produced a different key. Each change restarted the load and cancelled the one
  already running, so the work that finished was never the work that started.

  The key is now built from the *set* rather than the arrival order, so identical add-ons produce
  an identical key however they turn up. A genuine change — installing one, turning one off,
  reordering them — still invalidates it, because each add-on's position is part of what is
  compared. Measured on a Fire TV, a cold start went from four loads and two cancellations to one
  load followed by the ordinary fifteen-second refresh.

- **The sleep timer reporting a duration it was not using.** The two sleep settings offer 5, 10, 15,
  30, 60, 90, 120 and 240 minutes. The label only ever spelled out five of those values; every
  other choice — including all four of the short ones — fell through to a line that happened to
  read correctly in English and nowhere else. The label is now derived from the value itself: whole
  hours are said in hours, everything else in minutes, in your language, for every option in both
  lists and any added later. The timer's behaviour never changed; only what it claimed.

- **A settings page that reported success in one language and failure in another.** The line
  confirming a saved setting was built by gluing the setting's name onto a fragment. Where the name
  came from a translated row and the fragment did not, the result was half-translated. Both are now
  whole sentences with the setting's name as an argument.
