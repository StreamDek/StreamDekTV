# StreamDek TV v0.3.0

## What's New

- **Animation speed.** Under **Settings → Appearance**, four choices for how long transitions take
  across the whole app: **Off** changes state immediately and keeps only a brief crossfade so
  nothing snaps, **Fast** is shorter and subtler, **Standard** is the recommended balance and the
  default, and **Cinematic** is slower and fully visible. It applies as soon as you choose it, with
  nothing to back out of and no restart.

  Saved on this television and nowhere else. The setting used to live on your StreamDek account,
  which meant a speed chosen here arrived on every other device in the household — a television
  across the room and a phone at arm's length can reasonably want different answers, so it is now
  device-local. Your phone keeps its own, and the two apps agree on what each mode means.

  Reduced motion, whether the television's own setting or the one in **Settings →
  Accessibility**, takes priority over it. The row says so rather than reporting a speed the app is
  not using, and your choice is kept for when reduced motion is switched off again.

- **Getting to episode 176 without swiping there.** A season is a horizontal strip of cards, which
  is a fine way to look at twelve episodes and a poor way to reach the hundred-and-seventy-sixth.
  Seasons longer than twenty episodes are now cut into blocks of twenty: pick a block and the strip
  shows only that block, so no episode is ever more than nineteen cards from the start of the row.
  Seasons of twenty or fewer are unchanged — they do not need navigating.

- **Branded streaming network cards.** The Streaming Networks row can show each service's own
  artwork edge to edge instead of a logo on a white tile, with artwork for Netflix, Prime Video,
  Disney+, Paramount+, Peacock, Plex, Pluto TV, Shudder, Sky Go, SkyShowtime, Starz, YouTube and
  more. It follows the same account setting as the phone, so the two match.

- **External subtitles and subtitle delay on the Media3 player.** Both were previously available
  only on the other playback engine. A subtitle you have loaded from a file, or from an add-on, now
  displays on Media3 too, and its timing can be nudged when it runs ahead of or behind the dialogue.

## Improvements

- **The profile screen opens as one scene.** "Who's watching?" and the profile cards used to appear
  together, fully formed, the instant the screen was composed. The page is now revealed by a single
  choreographed sequence — the heading, then the line beneath it, then the cards from left to right
  — and the remote takes over the first card once it has arrived rather than a fixed moment later.
  Focus is handed over exactly once, so the cards still arriving cannot pull the highlight around,
  and the first button press lands the rest of the sequence immediately.

- **Home rows are grouped by where they come from.** The Home rows list folds into one section per
  source — StreamDek's own rows, and each add-on — so a long list is quick to scan. A source whose
  rows cannot currently appear is greyed and says why. Your individual row choices are kept exactly
  as they were and come back when the source is switched on again.

- **One motion system behind every animation.** Every transition in the app already took its timing
  from one place; that place now works from the same definitions of "quick", "standard" and "long"
  that the phone uses, which is what lets a single setting govern all of them without the two apps
  drifting apart. Loading indicators, playback and buffering timing, network timeouts, long-press
  thresholds and the sleep timers are deliberately untouched by it.

- **Refreshed launcher artwork and network hero branding.** New launcher icons — the previous set
  shipped the same 300KB image at every density — and tidier branding on the network hero.

## Fixed

- **An idle sleep timer that silently did nothing.** Neither the screensaver nor sleep is something
  an app is allowed to do on Android TV: both sit behind permissions the platform keeps for itself,
  and a retail Fire TV grants neither. The timer used to ask and assume it had worked. It now
  checks whether the request was actually honoured, and where it was not the app steps aside and
  leaves the set to its own idle timer instead of pretending the television went dark.

- **Focus returning to a card that was no longer there.** Moving back into a season or episode row
  aimed the highlight at whichever card it had been on last — including one the row had since
  scrolled away and stopped composing, which is not a quiet no-op but a crash. Rows now know which
  of their cards are actually on screen and send focus to the nearest one that is; where none is,
  the press becomes an ordinary focus search rather than an error. Pressing up from the episode row
  lands on a visible season chip for the same reason.
